# Tower Town 🏢

A cozy tower-town builder that runs in your browser. Inspired by Tiny Tower —
but instead of idle timers, it runs a **live simulation**: little residents
ride the lifts, go to work, climb career ladders, grab lunch, and even commute
between towers. Income comes from real foot traffic, so lift throughput is a
genuine gameplay constraint, not a cosmetic animation.

Free to play, no ads, no in-app purchases. Saves locally in your browser.

**Works offline.** It's an installable PWA: load it online once, then it runs
with no connection at all. On your phone or desktop, use your browser's "Add to
Home Screen" / "Install" option to launch it like a native app — no service
needed after the first visit.

## How to play

- **Keep the town in view**: the desktop dock keeps floor types, Landmarks,
  **Manage** and the town/tower switch in one row. Manage holds lift upgrades,
  Missions, Activity, Friends and New town; its button shows mission progress.
  Phones retain the three-button Build / Manage / Town dock. Menus focus the
  first available choice; Escape returns to their opening button, and clicking
  outside closes them without spending. Build notifications stay above the
  desktop subtype picker.
- **Get closer**: select a floor and choose **Explore this room** to inspect its
  people and furnishings. Use the zoom/pan buttons or drag the scene; arrow keys
  move across and between floors, plus/minus zoom, and Escape or **Full tower**
  restores the overview. Works in shared towns too, without editing them.
- **Your next chapter**: the journal keeps three ambitions in view—an improvement
  right now, a mission for this session, and a goal for your growing town. Open
  **Town journal** to meet neighbors and revisit their personal histories.
  On compact screens, the closed journal shows the current next action; tap it
  to open its destination, or use the goal count to expand all ambitions.
  Before the first arrival, it points to the ready apartments instead of another
  purchase and reminds you to resume the town if paused.
  Early cards introduce shopping, dining and work alongside homes; build cards
  open the relevant choices without spending until you select a floor. If funds
  are short, the card shows the amount still needed.
  Your first paying shop customer earns 60 milestone coins; your first paid meal
  earns 90. Each reward happens once, after arrival, and leaves a town memory.
  Those opening goals lead directly to a relevant shop or restaurant, preferring
  a staffed venue with actual arriving customers. The floor shows **Neighbors
  here** separately from **On their way**. Meet a diner or shopper through their
  portrait card, then return to the venue. These are real local residents and
  journeys, not extra scenery or predicted sales; event guests are not included
  in these neighbor lists. Up to six cards per group keep large crowds readable,
  with the full count still shown.
  Before ten neighbors, full homes take priority over optional renovations.
- **People remember**: promotions, new homes and difficult days become persistent
  stories. Neighbors who meet at work, meals or shops on three separate days form
  friendships. Resident portraits and thoughts help you see who needs attention.
  New place memories—first customers, critic reviews, signature businesses,
  studio expansions, concerts, festivals and landmark openings—link to the
  actual room or park, including from a neighbor's personal history. These
  destinations survive renaming, staff changes, saving and sharing. Older
  memories without a recorded location keep their existing resident links.
  New arrivals choose names not already used by current neighbors, including
  commuters; larger towns use first/family-name combinations. Saved residents
  keep their names. Directory cards also show their home tower and floor.
  Thoughts prioritize current concerns by their happiness impact and link to
  lift, workplace or wellbeing details without spending anything. Contented
  neighbors can mention their actual shift, meal, landmark visit, household cat
  or an established friend sharing the same activity and room. A normal full
  apartment no longer overrides every happy resident's daily life.
- **Follow a neighbor's concern**: individual housing, work, food and leisure
  meters show their own needs, not town averages. Meal and leisure concerns
  point to an eligible staffed business or public landmark in their home/work
  towers, or explain when an amenity is missing. Opening that destination keeps
  the neighbor's name, starting/current happiness and a **Back to** button in
  view. Navigation never dispatches them or improves mood by itself. Meals and
  daily happiness are reviewed at day's end; an actual recovery clears the
  move-out countdown and leaves a personal memory. Renovations still cost coins
  and improve business quality, not instant resident happiness.
- **Come back to people, not just totals**: after time away, the return report
  includes up to three recent town memories, net coin changes and completed
  missions. It explains the simulated-time cap and pauses the town while you
  read. Continue into the journal or return to the scene; rewards are already
  received, with nothing to claim.
  Catch-up follows trips in quarter-minute simulation steps and yields between
  short batches so its progress screen stays usable. **Return now** keeps only
  the progress already processed and skips the remaining time, as explained
  before you choose it. At most ten real hours are simulated at 1×.
- **Every floor has an opening**: its room, facade, lift shaft and rooftop rise
  together, furnishings appear, then the nameplate lights up with its own optional
  sound motif. Builds bring the new floor into view. Pause freezes the reveal;
  reduced motion shows the finished room immediately. People, pets, lift cabs,
  steam and queue signs wait for the room's visual reveal without delaying the
  underlying simulation's journeys or income.
- **Explore without covering the room**: close-up nameplates shrink onto the
  upper room edge, leaving furnishings, people and earned decorations visible.
  Roof-level plates sit below architectural brackets; entrance names mount on
  earned canopies, clear of accumulated snow. They adjust as new floors rise or
  you change architecture, without hiding the building's solid trim.
  The controls name the floor currently centered by the camera (or the rooftop).
  Full tower view and skyline postcards use the normal signs again.
- **Rooms have a daily rhythm**: real workers prepare coffee and cook; café
  and home walls pick up soft warm light at dusk when people are actually inside.
  Empty rooms stay subdued; a sleeping household dims while a night owl can
  keep the room warm. Small procedural light maps approximate this wall bounce
  without adding a shadow-casting light to every floor. Meanwhile, café
  customers collect their drinks and sit down; coworkers meet briefly at 10:00
  and 15:00 when at least two are actually present. At home, neighbors read in
  their own seats and lie down in separate beds after their individual bedtimes.
  A housemate leaving for work does not shuffle everyone else's seat or bed.
  Four-bed shared homes include a kitchenette, books, framed keepsakes and a
  board-game table; bedside lamps soften when everyone present is resting, while
  a night owl can keep reading. Some households
  have named cats on their windowsills—even while the owner commutes. Click a
  cat to meet its owner. Portraits and miniature residents share their colors.
  Reopening a paused town places residents directly in their current seats,
  beds, workplaces and lift queues; you do not need to resume to separate them.
- **Shops and workshops feel busy**: shoppers browse produce, inspect clothes or
  try devices, then move to checkout late in their existing visit when a clerk
  is present. Staff stock shelves, fold garments or demonstrate devices. Factory
  line workers use assembly stations, processing tanks or fabrication controls;
  the foreman inspects with a clipboard. These are real residents, not duplicate
  scenery actors, and the animation never creates extra purchases or production.
  These scenes do not invent staff, customers, rent, or extra visit income.
- **Shops look like their trade**: markets have produce bins, labeled jars and
  tiled floors; boutiques have wood floors, hanging garments and folded displays;
  electronics stores have boxed devices and laptop demonstration tables. Low
  counters and rear shelving keep the actual people visible. Track-light lenses
  brighten in occupied shops after dark and dim when empty.
- **Workshops have distinct equipment**: assembly plants use workbenches, vises
  and roller lines; food processors have stainless vats, valves, connected pipes
  and tiled drainage; electronics fabs have circuit-board workstations, extraction
  hoods and test cabinets. The three operators and foreman remain real residents,
  with clear work positions and lift access. Task-light lenses dim when empty.
- **Offices look lived in**: tech studios have server alcoves and coffee cups;
  law practices have reference libraries and case files; creative studios have
  material boards, swatches and sample storage. Three seated desks and a fourth
  standing workstation give the full staff room to work, with a separate meeting
  corner. Earned startup archives and prototypes sit behind the people, and
  pendant-light lenses soften when the room is empty after dark.
- **Restaurants have their own character**: coffee houses have wood floors and a
  slatted espresso counter; diners have tiled floors and red seating; fine dining
  has linen-covered tables and place settings; lounges have a bottle-lined bar,
  stools and brass details. Working kitchens have cabinets, tiled splashbacks and
  recognizable equipment. Chairs align with the real seated guests. Pendant and
  table lights warm up after dark while people are inside, then dim when empty.
- **Neighborhood happenings** grow from what you build. A thriving restaurant
  attracts a food critic. Serving the critic earns a permanent framed award,
  linen-and-brass table details and Critic’s choice signage. The award's picture
  light follows the room's occupancy after dark; the original dining seats and
  subtype remain. Social neighbors bring a band to the park; an established
  startup can expand into a second office. Its first room keeps a founders’ archive;
  the new Innovation hub gains a prototype bench and project board. Expansion
  updates both studios' staffing and open/closed appearance immediately,
  even while paused. Loaded and shared towns also show their assigned staffing
  before the clock runs; this does not move workers or grant wages in advance.
  The completed memory links to both offices, including after saving or sharing.
  Long-term friends can become neighborhood
  hosts with named welcome benches. Open their tower's lobby for readable host
  portrait cards and links to their stories, without targeting small scenery.
  Hosts share one +2 community mood bonus for their home tower; it does not stack.
  A park and busy storefronts unlock a festival:
  its guests use real lifts and pay only after reaching a business. Successful
  visits earn permanent special signs, folded fabric bunting and a framed festival
  rosette. The host keeps its own shop or restaurant furnishings; festival dressing
  does not add another counter or change its trade. Invitations wait for your
  response, and declining has no penalty. Concert bandstands remain after the show.
  Open event cards stay in place while counts and eligibility update; use the
  refresh button to reveal new invitations or completed memories when ready.
- **See the cost of congestion**: click **Lift flow** for live queues, stair
  journeys and missed business visits. Upgrades show measured before/after waits
  after ten trips as an early estimate, then completes the sample at twenty
  trips including abandonments. The comparison reports actual results rather than assuming
  every upgrade solved the problem.
  A newly purchased second shaft also reassigns existing queues by estimated
  pickup time; passengers already aboard stay put and nobody's wait clock resets.
  Urgent journal guidance follows current queues and recent trips, not an old
  wait average after everyone has gone home. After an upgrade, a temporary
  **Watch the change** card follows the real sample from 0 to 20 trips and shows
  the before/after result, including worse outcomes. It never grants a second
  reward or requires another purchase. Long live queues remain urgent while the
  sample is being collected. The three main goals, invitations and landmark
  choices take priority; the journal never exceeds five cards.
  The HUD, urgent lift goals, improvement cards and residents' lift concerns open
  the same dedicated Lift flow view directly, without scrolling past lobby
  customization. Its HUD chip labels historical averages separately from the
  number waiting now; no completed samples display a dash, not an invented zero.
  Keyboard focus enters the report, and observations keep updating while the
  panel itself is focused. Visiting a shared town remains read-only.
- **Make it yours**: **Our skyline** lets you name your town and districts, inspect
  attractiveness, and preview a 1600 × 1100 PNG postcard before downloading it.
  Postcards frame the whole built town independently of phone size or camera
  selection, with your town's names and statistics. Open a tower's lobby to rename it and
  choose unlocked architecture: textured brick piers and a water-tank roof,
  slim steel framing and glass fins, or timber screens, trailing planters and a
  rooftop pergola. Growth earns canopies, gardens and skyline art;
  thriving businesses can earn gold Signature signs.
- **Build a public landmark**: **Landmarks** in the Build menu (also **Our
  skyline**) offers a Sky Conservatory after 12 neighbors plus a park, a
  Neighborhood Gallery after 12 missions, and a Skyline Observatory after 32
  neighbors. The observatory also needs an eight-floor tower. Choose one permanent
  landmark per Mixed-Use or Transit-Oriented tower; it adds a new floor without
  replacing homes or jobs. Its cost is the base price plus 75 coins per existing
  floor. Residents living or working in that tower can take the lifts there after
  work: successful free visits restore entertainment and can grow friendships.
  Congestion can make them miss the outing. Each landmark adds 3 attractiveness
  points within the 20-point parks/public-places cap, with no admission income,
  jobs or upkeep. Visits, earned permits and chosen landmarks survive saves and
  shared-town codes. After dark, gallery display bars, conservatory reading strips
  and low amber observatory guides brighten occupied rooms and dim when empty.
- **Listen in**: **Sound on** enables a locally generated soundscape. Each
  business subtype and public landmark has its own short opening melody and
  instrument color. Zooming blends room hum with distant street/wind textures;
  evenings are livelier and late nights quieter. Rain is softer and more muffled
  close to rooms, snowfall quiets the street, and park music fades with distance.
  Sound starts only when enabled, and muting or hiding the tab stops queued notes.
- **Stay for sunset**: golden-hour shadows give way to a rose-and-blue horizon,
  then moonlight and stars. Occupied rooms light up with varied warm windows;
  empty rooms stay cool, and household lights soften toward bedtime. Restrained
  bloom and a soft front fill keep the little room scenes readable at night.
  Supported devices also smooth architectural edges before the glow is applied,
  keeping rails and furniture crisp without blurring the whole image. Smoothing
  scales down at large render sizes to limit extra graphics memory, including
  during postcard capture.
- **A setting beyond the street**: low, wooded hills give the skyline depth,
  fading into the same atmosphere as the town. Their meadow and winter snow
  match the foreground; warm twilight skylight keeps the landscape from turning
  abruptly dark. The backdrop is scenery, not extra lots, residents or income.
- **Watch the weather**: a saved seasonal forecast brings rain, umbrellas and wet
  street reflections, then winter snow that collects on roofs and melts gradually.
  Street commuters are visible all the way between towers. Rain and snow slow
  exposed walks; earned entrance canopies and transit-zone entrances reduce the
  delay. Actual coffee-shop visits earn 15% more during precipitation. Open the
  weather chip for the next forecast and an explanation. Sound adds sheltered or
  street-level rain; reduced-motion mode hides falling particles while keeping
  snow and wet-street cues. Pause also freezes precipitation, reflections,
  concert gestures and walking poses; resuming does not replay missed animation.
- **A neighborhood at street level**: a stone-paved pedestrian promenade joins
  the towers. Unlocked lots gain planted edges, slatted benches and warm street lamps;
  grouped broadleaf trees replace the old cone-shaped background trees. Saved
  snow accumulation whitens the ground and park lawns, caps tree crowns, and
  settles in planting beds. These details leave the existing walking route clear
  and do not change commute times, population or income.

- **Build floors** with the bottom menu: Apartments bring residents; Shops,
  Restaurants, and Offices give them places to spend and work. Each business
  gets a **subtype** you pick when building — grocery/boutique/electronics,
  coffee/fast-food/fine-dining, tech/law/creative — with different costs,
  income, and appeal to different resident tastes. New business names fit the
  chosen trade, from coffee houses and boutiques to law offices and circuit
  makers. Existing and player-edited names stay unchanged.
- **Businesses are real**: an unstaffed business is closed (gray sign, dim
  lights, no customers). Quality grows or decays daily based on staffing,
  traffic, and staff seniority; the inspector shows each floor's letter grade
  and daily profit & loss. Residents pick where to shop by quality and taste.
  Want to steer it directly? Open any business and **Renovate** it (spend coins
  for an instant quality bump) or **Promote** a tenured worker into an open
  senior role on the spot instead of waiting for the daily review.
- **Click anything**: floors open an inspector (rename them! see the grade and
  who works there), residents show their job title, traits, and happiness,
  empty lots show the price of your next tower.
- **Meet people without precision tapping**: apartment households, business staff
  and current landmark visitors have portrait buttons in the floor panel. Open
  a person's story, then use **Back to** to return to that floor. Staff lists show
  assignments and current activity, not a claim that everyone is physically there.
  These read-only controls also work while visiting a shared town.
- **Getting around**: clicking a tower locks the camera onto it head-on — just
  scroll up and down to see every floor, no fiddly orbiting. Small towers fit
  from street to rooftop; taller ones keep a readable scrolling window. With the
  scene keyboard-focused, use arrow/Page keys to scroll, **Home** for street level
  and **End** for the roof. Hit **Town view**
  to pull back and pan around the whole street, then click another tower to
  drop into it. Framing adapts to the screen and includes the next available lot;
  the journal collapses when resizing from a roomy desktop to a compact screen.
- **Careers are real**: residents start as Interns/Clerks/Servers and get
  promoted when tenure is met *and* a higher slot is free on their floor. Stay
  blocked too long and they'll jump ship to a promotion in another building —
  even another tower.
  Restaurant Chefs visibly lead the kitchen; a Server covering cooking does not
  borrow their senior uniform. Other staff carry serving trays or cover the café
  counter. Paid promotions add their personal memory immediately, even while
  paused, so saving before the next simulation tick does not lose the milestone.
- **Keep them happy**: residents track housing, employment, food, and
  entertainment needs, plus lift waits and commute length. Happy residents
  spend more; miserable ones eventually **move out**.
- **Manage the lifts**: one cab per shaft. Buy speed-and-capacity tiers, and —
  crucially for a tall, busy tower — add a **second shaft**: one cab simply
  can't serve a big tower even at top speed, and a second lift roughly triples
  throughput. Riders pick the shaft that will arrive first, and commuters leave
  home early enough to make their shift. A rider who waits too long **gives up
  and takes the stairs** rather than freezing in an endless queue, so the wait
  meter stays honest and recovers the moment you add capacity. If a tower is
  maxed on lifts and still jammed, it's simply too dense — spread residents
  across more towers, or zone a lot **Transit-Oriented**, whose lifts carry far
  more people per trip. (Tap the Happiness meter and it'll tell you when the
  lifts are your bottleneck.)
- **Grow the town**: unlock up to nine more lots along the street (ten in all)
  and raise a whole skyline of towers. Commute time scales with real distance
  between towers, so where you put the jobs matters.
- **Zone every new lot**: when you buy a lot you pick a municipal zone —
  Mixed-Use, Residential, Commercial, Office, Industrial, Transit-Oriented, or
  Open Space (a park) — and from then on you can only build *to code* there.
  Zoning is permanent, so plan the block. Your starting tower is Mixed-Use, and
  any town you built before zoning existed stays Mixed-Use, so nothing you
  already have becomes illegal.
- **Industry & supply chains**: Industrial lots host **Factories** (a new floor
  type with its own Line Worker → Foreman career). Staffed factories produce
  goods that give a quality boost to Commercial shops town-wide — electronics
  shops benefit most.
- **Parks**: an Open-Space lot builds no tower but lifts the mood of residents
  in nearby towers, and its lamp posts glow after dark.
- **Neighborhood invitations**: a critic notices a well-run restaurant, a band
  finds a welcoming park, or a local startup needs a bigger studio. Invitations
  explain why they happened and wait for your response; declining has no penalty.
  Accepted visits bring actual guests and venue-specific consequences, not
  imaginary crowds or arbitrary town-wide punishment. The ticker opens these
  choices even when the journal is collapsed. After dark a **hosted festival
  sets off fireworks** over the skyline: little golden rockets rise into soft,
  round bursts with falling trails, sized to stay delicate when zoomed in. They
  pause with the game and disappear when reduced motion is enabled. Occupied windows glow warm at night, the
  sky arcs from dawn through dusk to a starry night, and a sun and moon cross
  overhead — all with a soft cinematic bloom.
- **Evenings are alive**: after work, residents keep going out — dinner at a
  restaurant, a drink at a Bar & Lounge, some evening shopping — re-deciding
  through the night until their (staggered) bedtimes, so the town's busiest,
  liveliest stretch is actually the evening rather than a dead screen once the
  shops close. **Night owls** (about a third of residents) stay out late; every
  occupied room glows warm and park lamps come on after dark.
- **See what's wrong**: tap the Happiness meter for a breakdown of what's
  dragging the mood down (housing, jobs, food, entertainment, lift queues,
  commutes). Routine goings-on collect in an **Activity** feed instead of
  burying the screen in pop-ups.
  Happiness is reviewed at day’s end; travel diagnostics use current queues and
  respect transit zoning at either end of the commute. New apartments make room
  for arrivals, but do not automatically relocate existing households.
- **Celebrate without covering the town**: simultaneous milestones share one
  compact notice with their actual combined coin reward and new story-wall tiles.
  Use its arrow to read Missions or Activity; the reward is already received.
  Hover or focus the notice to keep reading, or dismiss it. New ambient news waits
  behind held notices and purchase/error feedback instead of making a tall stack.
  Activity separates recent town highlights from routine visits and hiring;
  earned milestones remain in Missions after the temporary notice expires.
- **Missions**: a checklist of 100+ milestones with coin rewards (🎯 under
  **Manage**) — population and wealth tiers, zoning goals, business grades, career
  promotions, happiness streaks, and more. Unfinished goals sort to the top.
  Every completed mission also adds a permanent colored enamel tile to the
  **Town story wall** in your founding tower's lobby. Open Missions or that
  lobby to read the recorded milestones and jump into the room to see the wall.
  It uses your existing saved completions, including when sharing a town, with
  no extra payment or duplicate reward.
  The chapter rail highlights a nearby session milestone using actual progress
  and unlock requirements; following its card puts that exact mission first.
  When a needed lift upgrade is out of reach, it can also offer an affordable
  staffed-business renovation as a choice. The card explains that those coins
  compete with lift savings and that renovation does not fix queues. On phones,
  **Compare an affordable option** reveals the tradeoff before any purchase.
  Daily goals explain their day-end check, completed streaks advance, and earned
  rewards are never offered twice. Creative district/Signature ambitions remain
  when the numeric milestones are exhausted. Daily earnings and active city
  conditions survive saves and visit codes, alongside friendships, memories,
  named places and earned skyline rewards. Unfinished trips replan on reload
  without treating an unserved purchase as completed.
- **Play with friends** (🌐 Friends): share a **visit code** and a friend can
  **walk through your town** read-only — scroll your towers, click floors and
  residents, watch it run — then hop back to their own. **Send coin gifts** as
  short codes too (e.g. `G3rs.k29fq1abc`): the coins come out of your treasury
  and land in theirs when they redeem it. It's all done with copy-paste codes,
  so there's still **no server, no account, and no tracking** — the game stays
  free, offline, and static.
  Gift balance changes and redemption records save together; a failed save leaves
  the balance and code unchanged. **Recent sent gifts** keeps the last 20 codes
  after their deductions are saved. Visit codes exclude this private outbox.
  Local replay protection retains the latest 500 redemption IDs, including across
  a successful New town reset; these offline codes are not server-authenticated.
  Friends and gift invitations pause the town while open. Their close button stays
  visible while the contents scroll; Escape closes without accepting a gift and
  returns keyboard focus to the opener. Closing resumes the selected game speed.
- **Speed controls**: pause / 1× / 2× / 4× (top right). Come back later and
  get a "while you were away" report — the town keeps living without you (up
  to a cap).
- Income: rent from every resident, tier-multiplied wages from workers actually
  at work, and per-visit income scaled by business quality and shopper mood.
- Progress saves automatically to `localStorage`. Failed writes show a persistent
  **Progress not saved** warning with retry and JSON-backup options. Saving options
  pause the town while open. Backup restoration currently requires manual help.
  An unreadable/unavailable save is not treated as a fresh start: choose **Retry
  load** or **Play without saving**, which protects the original and disables
  automatic writes and gifts. Successful **New town** resets cannot be undone by
  the outgoing page's unload autosave.

## Development

```bash
npm install
npm run dev      # dev server
npm test         # unit tests (elevator, economy, careers, town, save)
npm run build    # production build in dist/
npm run preview  # serve the production build
```

For isolated visual checks, open `/?preview=weather` on the development server.
This disposable, populated town has rain/snow/clear, morning/golden-hour/sunset/
dusk/blue-hour/night lighting and commute-replay
controls. It never reads or writes the player's saved town and is excluded from
the production bundle.

`/?preview=neighborhood` provides a separate populated town with eligible event
invitations. Its **Next town evening** control advances the simulation through
normal minute-by-minute trips and daily reviews. **Advance one evening hour**
gives a shorter step for following actual guests toward event rewards. It never
accesses a saved town and is excluded from production.

`/?preview=hosts` restores two paid welcome-bench dedications from a detached
save. Friendship history and age are staged; invitations and charges use normal
game actions. Select either named bench to meet its host. The scene is disposable
and excluded from production, with no access to player saves.

`/?preview=opening` starts an ordinary empty town with normal coins and prices.
Pause, then use **Advance 15 seconds at 1×** to follow real move-ins, customer
trips and early milestones. It stages no residents, purchases or rewards, never
accesses a player save, and is excluded from production.

`/?preview=construction` stages an eligible, populated town but builds the
selected floor through normal purchases. Playback can hold its structure,
furnishings or lit-sign phase. **Opening motion** follows the system by default;
study-only full/reduced overrides affect this construction reveal alone. The
status reports both the effective mode and actual browser preference. Reduced
motion completes an active reveal immediately; returning to full does not replay
it. Other effects still follow the system, and production has no override.
**Advance to next
workday** runs ordinary quarter-minute simulation ticks to 10:00 the following
day so hiring and arrivals can be inspected without staging workers or customers.
It never accesses a player save and is excluded from production.

`/?preview=return` stages buildings, runs real move-ins and trips, restores a
detached save, then simulates four minutes away through the actual catch-up
path. It shows the normal return dialog without accessing player saves or
autosaving. **Study ten-hour absence** repeats the disposable study at the cap
to inspect progress, early return, and completion. Accuracy tests separately
compare identical restored towns against fine-step simulation; large-town and
real-phone catch-up performance still need measurement.

`/?preview=saving` exercises failed writes, recovery and load-error choices using
the real save functions and UI against disposable in-memory storage. **Read saved
snapshot** verifies the previous/recovered snapshot without replacing the running
town. Reload starts fresh; this is not a browser-storage reload test. It never
accesses a player save, and all study controls are excluded from production.
Its gift-study buttons use the same in-memory store to test sending and invitation
acceptance under blocked/recovered writes. Generated codes are test data, not
gifts to distribute to other players.

`/?preview=persistence` uses actual browser storage in a separate, development-only
namespace. It runs the normal startup, edit-save, autosave, unload, reset and
catch-up paths without reading or writing player-save keys. The town starts with
normal coins and no staged residents or rewards; it stays frozen between explicit
15-second simulation steps. **Save checkpoint**, **Read stored checkpoint** and
**Reload study** make real reload comparisons possible. Unlike the memory study,
its test town survives reload. **New town** stays in the study and resets only its
own save. Gift codes generated here are test data, not gifts to distribute.

`/?preview=rooms` is an isolated room study with room and lighting selectors and a replay
button. Its restaurant selector compares coffee, diner, fine-dining and lounge
interiors on the same floor. Office selectors compare tech, law and creative
interiors, plus the founders' archive and innovation-hub displays. Reward choices
stage appearance only; they do not verify earning the startup event rewards.
It stages actual residents at work, visiting cafés,
and at home, within normal staffing and career-slot capacities. After switching
or replaying, resume briefly so people reach their new positions; pause to inspect.
Selecting a home brings its existing four-person household into that room without
changing their jobs. **Late evening** compares three resting neighbors with one
night owl; **After midnight** shows all four in bed. These are staged visual
studies, not a substitute for ordinary overnight gameplay or save/reload checks.
This preview is also excluded from production and never accesses a player save.

Its **Prepare promotion study** button stages Maya and Fern as Servers, with
only Fern eligible by tenure. Open Juniper Table and use its ordinary **Promote**
button to check the real charge, immediate story and kitchen handover. The study
does not grant the promotion itself; resume to let staff reach their new stations.

`/?preview=landmarks` provides three eligible towers and a park. Build each public
landmark through the real UI; its five- and ninety-minute evening controls run
ordinary quarter-minute simulation ticks. The visit readout reports real present
neighbors, entertainment and visit/missed counts. Prepare a read-only shared-town
link to inspect the actual saved reconstruction. Population and milestones are
staged; arrivals and purchases are not. It never accesses the player's save and
is excluded from the production build.

`/?preview=landmark-save` adds isolated, persistent browser storage to the same
eligible town. Buy landmarks normally, advance actual evening trips, then use
**Save checkpoint** and **Reload landmark study**. Its readout compares purchased
floors, architecture, permits, visitors and their entertainment. It starts frozen;
**Resume study** enables ordinary time and opening animations. This study uses a
separate allowlisted save namespace, including resets and gifts, without touching
player progress or the earlier persistence study. It is development-only; the
ordinary landmark preview remains disposable.

`/?preview=sound` extends the disposable weather study with an opening-cue
selector for every business subtype, apartments and all three landmarks. Enable
**Sound on**, then use **Play opening cue**; switch weather, daylight and camera
views to compare the ambience. The panel reports the real audio engine state
and active/scheduled tones. It does not play anything automatically, access a
player save, or ship in production. Subjective listening is still needed to tune
the mix across headphones, phone speakers and desktop speakers.

`/?preview=trades` is a disposable shop/workshop study with all six retail and
factory subtypes plus browsing/checkout phases and day/dusk/night lighting. Pause, then replay or change the
room to inspect real resident staging; resume to watch gestures and visits unfold.
It does not access player saves and is excluded from the production build.

For rendering comparisons, open `/?preview=landmarks&metrics=paused`. This freezes
simulation and scene animation from startup. Select the same camera view and let
the measurements settle. The landmark study uses a repeatable weather seed;
resident names and traits are still generated. Add `&unbatched-exteriors=1` to
compare facade trim, shaft rails, rooftop shells and park furniture without
exterior batching. The separate `&unbatched=1` switch disables procedural-room
batching. Use `metrics=1` for a live study instead. The visible panel includes
all shadow/post-processing draw calls, triangles, uploaded resources and recent
frame/CPU percentiles. Browser automation can throttle frames: this is not a
real-phone benchmark or GPU timing. These switches are development-only; use
disposable previews, not a player save, for comparisons.

`/?preview=transit` stages real rush-hour journeys with accumulated wait history.
Pause, then use **Follow next stair departure** to advance ordinary simulation
ticks until a resident abandons their queue (up to ninety game minutes).
**Advance a quarter minute** follows actual progress on the alternating flights.
The visible status names current stair users and their destinations. This is a
disposable development study; it does not access or modify a saved town.

Built with [Three.js](https://threejs.org/), TypeScript, and Vite. The whole game
is static files — deploy `dist/` anywhere (GitHub Pages, itch.io, Vercel).

## Architecture

```
src/
  core/      # engine-independent simulation
    town.ts       # shared clock+wallet, tower slots, commute handoffs
    game.ts       # one tower: floors, lift shafts, residents inside it
    careers.ts    # hiring, tenure promotions, poach-switching
    elevator.ts   # per-shaft cab simulation: queueing, capacity, dispatch
    economy.ts    # traffic-driven income (visits, wages, rent)
    residents.ts  # daily-schedule "brain" incl. cross-tower commuting
  render/    # Three.js dollhouse cross-sections: floors, people, cabs, plots, parks
  input/     # raycast picking that coexists with the orbit camera
  ui/        # DOM overlay: HUD, build menu, inspector, toasts
  main.ts    # game loop wiring it all together
```

The `core/` modules have no Three.js dependency, so the simulation is fully
unit-testable (see `src/core/*.test.ts`).

Custom 3D models: drop `.glb` files under `public/models/` and point the
manifest in `src/render/assets.ts` at them — anything missing falls back to
the built-in procedural geometry automatically.
